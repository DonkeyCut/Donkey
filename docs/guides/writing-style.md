Write a technical article in the style of a thoughtful engineer sharing something they learned while building real software.

The writing should be:

* Conversational, calm, and technically precise
* Simple without being dumbed down
* Curious and analytical rather than authoritative
* Focused on explaining *why* something works, not just *what* to do
* Concrete, with real examples and small pieces of code where useful

Start with a real problem or observation. Build a mental model from first principles, then use that model to explain the solution and its tradeoffs.

Avoid:

* Marketing language
* Generic introductions
* “In this article, we will...”
* Excessive headings and bullet points
* Unnecessary jargon
* Repetitive summaries
* AI-sounding phrases
* Overly polished or corporate writing

Make it feel like an experienced engineer thinking through a problem in public. The reader should finish understanding *why the system behaves the way it does*, not just memorizing a solution.
